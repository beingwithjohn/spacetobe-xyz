A practice map

        
Exploring the body–heart–mind.

        

          

            
This is a map, not a method. It describes territory — what the body, the heart and the mind actually are as places to rest attention — so that you can find your own way.

            
Nothing here asks you to believe anything. Every claim is written to be tested against your own experience, and to be let go of if it doesn't land.

          

          
No experience needed. Nothing to believe.

The map is not the territory.

    


    
    

      

        
The rhythm

        
Effort and rest.

        
Each territory has two ways of approach. First you might cultivate — a deliberate practice, something to do. Then you might rest — stop efforting, and let what is already here reveal itself.

        
They are not stages, and you don't graduate from one to the next. You already have what these practices point at: presence, resonance and spaciousness are uncovered, not manufactured.

      

      

        
The balance

        
Two qualities hold a practice in balance. When they come into tune, a third follows on its own.

      

      

        

          ◇
          
Gentleness

          
A softening. A way of treating ourselves and others as precious, and a counter to the harshness that can creep in when we apply effort. Without it, practice becomes another place where we're hard on ourselves.

        

        

          ◇
          
Precision

          
An exactness. A way of meeting experience with complete contact, and a counter to the dullness that can arise when we grow complacent. Without it, practice can become a place to hide from what's true.

        

      

    


    

      
What follows

      
Letting go.

      
Not a technique but a result: right effort, the absence of aggression, the natural release of clinging. It happens naturally, once gentleness and precision are in tune.

    


    
    
    

      

        
Grounding

        
The body

        
The body is always in contact with the present. It is a field of changing sensation with its own non-conceptual intelligence — grounded, immediate, already here.

        
But we rarely stay with it. We fix our attention elsewhere, often to avoid what feels uncomfortable, and lose awareness of that which is never anywhere but now.

      

      

        

          Cultivate
          

            
Turn toward sensation

            
Body scans and walking meditation. Allow your attention to fill the body, deepening your capacity to be with the pleasant, the unpleasant and the neutral alike.

          

        

        

          Rest
          

            
Let awareness diffuse

            
Rest your attention. Let the body's natural sensitivity become a ground you relax into, and trust its intelligence to hold your life.

          

        

      

    

    

      
What is revealed

      
Presence.

      
You don't have to do being here. You already are. What's left is embodiment without striving — a trust that the body can be rested in, exactly as it is.

    


    
    
    

      

        
Resonating

        
The heart

        
The heart is an organ of attunement. It resonates — with our own experience, with others, with the wider world — and it carries its own knowing: a reservoir of care, compassion and courage.

        
Its capacity to feel is endlessly deep, and because it is so deep, we often find our hearts closing to protect us from the fullness of it. Opening the heart is not done by force.

      

      

        

          Cultivate
          

            
Wish well, on purpose

            
Loving-kindness. Deliberate warmth, turned toward yourself and then outward, until gentleness begins to feel natural rather than performed.

          

        

        

          Rest
          

            
Let the heart resonate

            
Begin in the body, then bring awareness to the heart and let its qualities respond on their own.

          

        

      

    

    

      
What is revealed

      
Resonance.

      
You are not generating the care. You are discovering that the heart does this on its own — it resonates and responds, if you let it. Warmth reaches as readily to an animal, a tree or a place as to a person.

    


    
    
    

      

        
Opening

        
The mind

        
The mind thinks — that is what it does. Usually we don't notice we're thinking; we're simply lost to it.

        
Our culture teaches us to meet a complex world through concept and abstraction, and that never quite satisfies, because the world is not a concept. It is an unfathomable, changing reality.

      

      

        

          Cultivate
          

            
Note thinking as thinking

            
When you notice you've been thinking, note it, and return. Thought becomes one phenomenon among many — not reality itself.

          

        

        

          Rest
          

            
Open into space

            
Through body, then heart, then a recognition of the mind as naturally spacious. Let phenomena arise and pass by themselves.

          

        

      

    

    

      
What is revealed

      
Spaciousness.

      
The noticing was always happening in something already open. Thoughts arise and pass without your control; you don't have to run the show. What remains is a sense of spaciousness, and rest.

    


    
    
    

      

        
On the posture

        
Some suggestions on form

        
Find the same balance in physical form: precision with gentleness.
Wakeful and at ease, at the same time.

      

      

        
Place
Indoors is good. Outdoors can be better — a garden, a doorway, a park bench, or simply the window open. Weather, birdsong and traffic can be part of practice.

        
Seat
Chair, cushion, bench or floor — all of them work. Aim to have your hips a little higher than your knees, so the lower back keeps its natural curve.

        
Spine
Upright without strain. Think of lengthening from the crown of the head. Uprightness here is dignity, not rigidity.

        
Hands
Resting palms down on the thighs or cradled in the lap.

        
Face
Relaxed muscles of the face, mouth closed or lips just separated, tongue soft. Breathe through the nose if you can.

        
Eyes
Open with a soft, unfocused gaze a metre or so ahead, or closed. Try both over a stretch of days; they produce noticeably different practices.

        
Pain
Move. Look after your body. Enduring real pain teaches nothing except that practice hurts. Lie down, stand, walk. Do what you have to do.

      

    


    
    
    

      

        
The point of a map

        
Making it your own

        
A map is no use if you only ever read it. The three territories are yours to explore in whatever order and rhythm your practice asks for. Let your practice become a friend.

        
Twenty minutes is standard, and sitting daily matters far more than sitting long. On a hard day, five minutes counts. When you lapse — everyone lapses — simply begin again. Beginning again is not a failure. It is the practice.
